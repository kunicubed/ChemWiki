**Tollens' reagent** is a solution of the **diamminesilver(I) ion**,
$\ce{[Ag(NH3)2]+}$. It's a mild oxidising agent used to test for **aldehydes**.
A positive result is a shiny **silver mirror** on the inside of the test tube.

## Making it

Tollens' reagent doesn't keep, so it's made fresh just before use:

1. Add a few drops of sodium hydroxide to silver nitrate solution. A brown
   precipitate of silver(I) oxide forms.
2. Add dilute ammonia drop by drop until the precipitate just dissolves.

$$
\ce{Ag2O + 4NH3 + H2O -> 2[Ag(NH3)2]+ + 2OH-}
$$

## The test

Add the sample and warm the tube gently in a water bath.

| Sample | Result |
|---|---|
| Aldehyde | Silver mirror (or black/grey precipitate) |
| Ketone | No change |

The aldehyde is oxidised to a carboxylate ion, and silver(I) is reduced to silver
metal:

$$
\ce{RCHO + 2[Ag(NH3)2]+ + 3OH- -> RCOO- + 2Ag + 4NH3 + 2H2O}
$$

Ketones don't react because there's no hydrogen on the carbonyl carbon, so they
can't be easily oxidised.

Reducing sugars like glucose also give a silver mirror, because their open-chain form
has an aldehyde group.

## Safety

Leftover Tollens' reagent must be poured away straight after the test (with lots of
water). If it's left to stand it can form **silver nitride**, which is explosive
when dry.
