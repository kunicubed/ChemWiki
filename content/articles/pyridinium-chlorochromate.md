**Pyridinium chlorochromate**, or **PCC**, is a mild oxidising agent. Its big
advantage is that it oxidises a **primary alcohol to an aldehyde and stops there**,
without going on to the carboxylic acid.

## What it does

| Alcohol | Product with PCC |
|---|---|
| Primary | Aldehyde |
| Secondary | Ketone |
| Tertiary | No reaction |

$$
\ce{CH3CH2CH2OH ->[\text{PCC}][\ce{CH2Cl2}] CH3CH2CHO}
$$

## Why it stops at the aldehyde

With aqueous oxidising agents like acidified [[Potassium dichromate]], the aldehyde
reacts with water to form a **hydrate**, $\ce{RCH(OH)2}$. The hydrate has an OH group
that can be oxidised again, giving the carboxylic acid.

PCC is used in **dry dichloromethane**, with no water present. No hydrate forms, so
the aldehyde can't be oxidised further. This means you don't need to distil the
aldehyde out as it forms.

## Safety

PCC contains chromium(VI), so it's **toxic and carcinogenic** like dichromate. Chromium
waste is also hard to dispose of. Many labs now use chromium-free alternatives,
such as the **Swern oxidation** or **Dess–Martin periodinane**, which do the same job.
