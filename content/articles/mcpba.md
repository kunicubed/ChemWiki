**mCPBA** (**meta-chloroperoxybenzoic acid**) is a **peroxyacid**. It has an
O–O bond, $\ce{-CO-O-OH}$, and the outer oxygen is easy to transfer to other
molecules. Its two main uses are making **epoxides** from alkenes and turning
**ketones into esters**.

## Epoxidation of alkenes

mCPBA adds one oxygen atom across a C=C double bond to make an **epoxide**, a
three-membered ring with one oxygen:

$$
\ce{RCH=CHR + ArCO3H -> RCH(O)CHR + ArCOOH}
$$

where Ar is the 3-chlorophenyl group. The by-product is 3-chlorobenzoic acid.

The reaction happens in **one step** (it's concerted), with both new C–O bonds forming
on the same face of the alkene. So it's **stereospecific**:

- a *cis* alkene gives a *cis* epoxide
- a *trans* alkene gives a *trans* epoxide

Epoxides are useful because the strained ring opens easily, so they can be turned
into diols, amino alcohols and lots of other things.

## Baeyer–Villiger oxidation

mCPBA can also insert an oxygen atom next to the C=O of a ketone, turning it into an
**ester**. A cyclic ketone becomes a lactone (a cyclic ester).

Which side the oxygen goes in depends on which group is better at moving
(migrating). Roughly:

$$
\text{tertiary} > \text{secondary} \approx \text{phenyl} > \text{primary} > \text{methyl}
$$

So for a methyl ketone, the oxygen ends up on the side of the bigger group.

## Safety

Like other peroxides, pure mCPBA can explode from shock or heat. It's sold at about
**70–77% purity**, mixed with 3-chlorobenzoic acid and water to make it safer.
