**Lithium aluminium hydride**, usually written **LiAlH₄** or **LAH**, is one of the
strongest reducing agents used in organic chemistry. It reduces almost every type of
carbonyl group, so it is the go-to reagent when a milder one like
[[Sodium borohydride]] isn't strong enough.

## What it does

LiAlH₄ is a source of the **hydride ion**, $\ce{H-}$. The hydride acts as a
nucleophile and attacks the δ+ carbon of a C=O bond. In equations the reducing agent
is often just written as $\ce{[H]}$.

| Starting compound | Product |
|---|---|
| Aldehyde | Primary alcohol |
| Ketone | Secondary alcohol |
| Carboxylic acid | Primary alcohol |
| Ester | Two alcohols |
| Amide | Amine |
| Nitrile | Primary amine |

It does **not** reduce a normal C=C double bond.

## Reactions

Reducing a carboxylic acid to a primary alcohol:

$$
\ce{CH3COOH + 4[H] -> CH3CH2OH + H2O}
$$

Reducing a nitrile to an amine:

$$
\ce{CH3CN + 4[H] -> CH3CH2NH2}
$$

The reaction is done in **dry ether** first. Water or dilute acid is added only
afterwards (the *work-up*) to protonate the product and destroy any leftover LiAlH₄.

## Why it's so reactive

The Al–H bond is quite polar, and aluminium is less electronegative than boron.
This makes the hydride in LiAlH₄ much more "available" than in
[[Sodium borohydride|NaBH₄]], so it can even attack weak electrophiles like the
carbonyl in esters and carboxylic acids.

## Safety

LiAlH₄ reacts **violently with water**, giving off flammable hydrogen gas:

$$
\ce{LiAlH4 + 4H2O -> LiOH + Al(OH)3 + 4H2}
$$

It has to be kept completely dry and is never used in water or alcohol solvents.
