**Thionyl chloride**, **SOCl₂**, is a fuming liquid used to swap an **OH group for a
Cl atom**. It's the cleanest way to make acyl chlorides and chloroalkanes.

## Reactions

### Alcohols → chloroalkanes

$$
\ce{CH3CH2OH + SOCl2 -> CH3CH2Cl + SO2 + HCl}
$$

### Carboxylic acids → acyl chlorides

$$
\ce{CH3COOH + SOCl2 -> CH3COCl + SO2 + HCl}
$$

Acyl chlorides are very reactive, so this is usually the first step in making esters
and amides from carboxylic acids.

## Why it's useful

The by-products, $\ce{SO2}$ and $\ce{HCl}$, are both **gases**. They just bubble off,
so you're left with a fairly pure product, and any extra SOCl₂ can be boiled off
because it's volatile.

Compare the other ways of doing the same job:

| Reagent | By-products |
|---|---|
| $\ce{SOCl2}$ | $\ce{SO2(g)}$, $\ce{HCl(g)}$ |
| $\ce{PCl5}$ | $\ce{POCl3(l)}$, $\ce{HCl(g)}$ |
| $\ce{PCl3}$ | $\ce{H3PO3}$ |

With $\ce{PCl5}$ and $\ce{PCl3}$ the by-product has to be separated off afterwards.

## Safety

Thionyl chloride reacts **violently with water** and fumes in moist air:

$$
\ce{SOCl2 + H2O -> SO2 + 2HCl}
$$

Both gases are toxic and corrosive, so it's only used in a fume cupboard, with dry
glassware.
